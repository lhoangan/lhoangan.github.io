Selected

  • Project page
    • Three for one and one for three: Flow, Segmentation, and Surface Normals
    Optical flow, semantic segmentation, and surface normals represent different information modalities, yet together they bring better cues for scene understanding problems. In this paper, we study the influence between the three modalities: how one impacts on the others and their efficiency in combination. We employ a modular approach using a convolutional refinement network which is trained supervised but isolated from RGB images to enforce joint modality features. The experimental results show positive influence among the three modalities, especially for objects' boundaries, region consistency, and scene structures. To assist the training process, we also provide a large-scale synthetic outdoor dataset that supports dense annotation of semantic segmentation, optical flow, and surface normals. .
    Hoang-An Le, Anil Baslamisli, Thomas Mensink, Theo Gevers
  • Project page
    • Joint Learning of Intrinsic Images and Semantic Segmentation
    In this paper, we combine the tasks of semantic segmentation and intrinsic image decomposition to explore their mutal relationship. To this end, we propose a supervised end-to-end convolutional neural network architecture to joinly learn intrinsic image decomposition and semantic segmentation. We analyze the gains of addressing those two problems jointly. The experiments show that joint tasks although being trained on synthetic dataset can benefit and improve each others in real world images. .
    Anil S. Baslamisli, Thomas Tiel Groenestege, Partha Das, Hoang-An Le, Sezer Karaoglu, Theo Gevers
  • Project page
    • CNN based Learning using Reflection and Retinex Models for Intrinsic Image Decomposition
    This paper exploits the best of both deep learning and tradition approaches on intrinsic image decomposition. A method is proposed that (1) is empowered by deep learning capabilities, (2) considers a physics-based reflection model to steer the learning process, and (3) exploits the traditional approach to obtain intrinsic images by exploiting reflectance and shading gradient information.
    Anil S. Baslamisli, Hoang-An Le, Theo Gevers

    About

    My name is Hoang-An Le (with full diacritics in Vietnamese, Lê Hoàng Ân). I was born in Ho Chi Minh city, Vietnam.

    Starting from April 2016, I am a PhD student at Computer Vision group, University of Amsterdam in the Netherlands. I am working with Professor Theo Gevers on the project TrimBot2020, funded by EUHorizon. My thesis topic focuses on 3D semantic segmentation.

    In 2015, I received my Master diploma of Science in Engineering (diplôme d'Ingénieur, an advanced academic degree awarded only by French prestigious elite universities, or Grandes Écoles) from Télécom ParisTech and institut de Mines-Télécom, France. My study was pursued at Campus SophiaTech, EURECOM institute, specialized in Multimedia Communications. My graduation project was "Multi-Camera Matching and Point Clouds Registration for 3D Immersive Visual Systems", carried out at Coordinated Science Lab, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign (UIUC), USA, under the supervision of Professor Minh Do.

    I received the Bachelor of Science degree in 2012 from the Advanced Program in Computer Science at Ho Chi Minh University of Science (HCMUS), Vietnam National University (VNU). The program is a join program between HCMUS and Portland State University (PSU), USA, in which all courses are adapted from PSU syllabus and offered in English. I have started doing scientific research since the sophomore year, and joined a research group led by Professor Minh-Triet Tran. Under his supervision, I had done my graduation project, entitled "Natural User Interface for Smart Environment".
