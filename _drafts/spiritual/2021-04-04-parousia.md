## Lesson 2: the Covenant
- the idea at the foundation of the Bible, the bond that makes us the family of God on earth
  - covenant = testament = diatheke (G) = berith (H)
- what is a covenant
  - an agreement that establishes or restores a family bond where none had previously
  existed or one had been broken: marriage, adoption
  - not the same as contract
    - covenant: exchange persons ("I am yours and you are mine")
    - contract: exchange goods or services ("This is yours and this is mine")
    - covenant includes a contract, because covenant involves obligations towards another
    - covenant is sealed by a solemn oath, not just a promise or signature on a piece of paper
      - God is called to witness and to enforce the terms of the relationship
      - lifelong and unbreakable
  - terms of covenant:
    - swearing an oath
    - declaring the terms of relationship
    - signing the agreement
    - establishes a sign of the new relationship
- covenant of creation, a covenant with creatures:
  - God has done this with humanity, making them not merely servants but sons and daughters
  - God fulfills all the ancient requirement of a covenant
    - Adam: sign = the Sabbath
    - Noah: sign = rainbow
    - Abraham: sign = circumcision
    - Israel: sign = Passover
    - David and his lineage: sign = kingship
  - Each is the renewal of the first covenant:
    - covenant of creation, of marriage of adam and eve: a family bond between God and his creatures
    - God first gave kinship to married couple, Adam and Eve
    - Extending to a household, Noah
    - Then the tribe of Abraham,
    - Then the nation of Israel, 12 tribes
    - Finally to the kingdom of David
  - Each new covenant came with renewed law and renewed order or worship
    - profound effect on the liturgy, Biblica region is covenantal
    - God seals and renews all the biblical covenants by liturgical signs
  - The purpose of covenants:
    - All the covenant prefigur and foreshadow a covenant the would be definitive and new and eternal
    - This covenant is sealed not by animals' blood by the blood of the Son of God
    - It is so important, hence Jesus uses it with maximum effect, only once at the Last Supper
  - The passover meal:
    - The meal that seals God's covenant with Moses, fulfilling liturgical command of the Old Conv.
    - Also changing it by his own divine authority: this passover is different from all those
    that had gone before
    - This cup which is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood
      - The son of God is establishing a new covenant, offering his own body and blood in
      the appearances of bread and wine
    - the Bread of Life discourse, in an early passover (a year or 2 before)
      - Synagogue in Capernaum
      - John 6:49-58, invoke the Old Covenant and contrast it with the sign of the new covenant
      - to be in covenant with God, then is to have life in its fullness, to live forever in God's
      family and to share his table
      - to consume the bread of life is more than just to have something more than a relationship
      with God, but to have communion with him, to be united with him
      - 2 Peter 1:4 it is to become partakers of the divine nature
      - The Jews was shocked and many of his disciples backed down and no longer followed him
      - But Jesus never hedges his statement or qualified his terms, or said, if it was
      only symbolically or metaphorically. These are the non-negotiable terms of the New Covenant
    - The mandatory sign of the covenant was the sharing of Jesus' body and blood under
    the appearances of bread and wine
    - This ritual which Jesus instiuted at the Last Supper was to be re-enacted throughout the
    rest of history
    - "Do this in memory of me": memory is an essential part of every covenant renewal
      - a memorical sacrifice = a remembrance of the terms of the covenant, a reminder of Israel's
      identity as the family of God
      - The Jews didn't think of memory the way we do today: not the purely psychological act
      of recalling the past, not nostalgia
      - It was about making the past present, again and again
    - E.g. Passover as Exodus
      - renewed and direct experience of the Exodus
      - the children of Israel of every generation were expected to participate
      in their national's primordial libration (they have to travel to Jerusalem)
    - This new ritual established by the Lord would be the fulfillment of all the
    rituals of the Old Covenant
      - Jesus's sacrifice rendered Israel's previous ceremonial laws obsolete like
      prophecies that were fulfilled
    - It does not, however, dispensed entirely with ritual worship
      - All Israel's traditional worship were like mighty rivers that flowed into the
      infite ocean of adoration that Jesus established for the Church, the Divine
      Worship of the New Covenant: they didn't vanish there, the found completion;
      they weren't abolished, they were fulfilled
      - All the older covenants were recapitualated by Jesus in the New
  - From the covenant to Communion:
    - Saint Paul: all the language of convenant in the Old Testament gives way to the language
    of Communion
    - the Mass : Koinonia (G) = Communion or Participation
    - Communion in the blood of Christ: "the cup of blessing which we bless is it not
    a participation in the blood of Christ" (1 Corin 10:16)
    - Becase there is one Bread, we who are many are one body for we all partake in the one bread
  - Shattered boundaries
    - Saint Paul makes explitcit what was implicit in Jesus' Bread of Life Discourse
    - The Lord's salvation has shattered not only the boundaries between Israel and the nations
    but also between God and the World
    - Communion is now possilbe between God and humanity, also among all peoples, both Jews and
    Gentiles
    - God's family is now, at long last, universal, Catholic
- The new Covenant
  - is indeed something new and ternal, great, and glorious
  - but it also the continuity of the past
  - **Israel's ritual worship was ordered to covenant remembrance which took place
  in reading, and a covenant renewal which took place in sacrifice**
  - Christian worship, also, is a remembrance of God's mighty works in history
  through Jesus' saving passion, ressurection and glorification
  - The Christian Eucharist remains both a covenant renewal and a thanksgiving for
  God's continued presences among his people
  - That presence, **PAROUSIA** is a true communion, Christ comes to abide in us and we
  come to abide in him
  - We thus, become divinized or deified, become sons in the Son, "the children of God"
  throught the communion with the only Son of God
- All the language of Sacred Scripture is covenantal and all the signs of the liturgy are convenantal
  - We can't understand the Bible, the Old or the New apart from the convenantal liturgy
  - We can't understand the Mass, without an understanding of the biblical notion of covenant.


## Lesson 3: the New Testament: document or sacrament

- Where in the Bible do you find the sacrifice of the Mass
  - Calvary is a sacrifice alright, but the Mass is just a meal
- Jesus's sacrifice on the cross is one and for all and there is no saving sacrifice
apart from the cross, but there is nothing about what happened on Calvary that would have
suggested sacrifice to any first-centural Jews
  - No devout Jews witnessing Jesus' crucifixion on Good Friday would have gone home
  and said that they had just witnessed a sacrifice
  - For Jews, a sacrifice must:
    - take place inside the Temple
    - on top of an altar
    - with a Levitical priest presiding
  - Jesus' crucifixion:
    - took place outside the walls,
    - no Temple, no altar, no priest
    - look nothing more than a bloody Roman execution
- From Execution to Sacrifice
  - Transformative answer = Jesus' offering of his body and blood at the Last Supper
  - Because Jesus spoke of that offering in sacrifical terms:
    - he called it the New Covenant in his Blood, echoiing the words of Moses,
    - Moses had ratified the old covenant at Mt. Sinai with a sacrifice:
    - "Behold the blood of the convenant which the Lord has made with you" (Exodus)
  - The apostles in turn looked upon Jesus' memorial in liturgical and sacrificial terms
  - "For Christ, our paschal lamb has been sacrificed" (1Con5:7)
  - So Holy Thursday is what transformed Good Friday from an execution into a Sacrifice
  - Easter Sunday is what transformed that sacrifice into the Blessed Sacrament
    - Because now Christ's Body is raised in glory, and by the power of the Holy Spirit,
    it's communicable to the faithful
    - It is the same sacrifice that Jesus offered when he instituted the Eucharist
    and then dying on Calvary, only now, his sacred humanity is glorified deified and deifying to us
    - It is the high-priestly sacrifice that he offers in heaven and on earth
    - That is the holy sacrifice of the Mass.
- If the Eucharist were only a meal, then Calvary would only be an execution
- SO where in the New Testament is the Sacrifice of the Mass
  - **The Sacrifice of the Mass is the New Testament**
  - The New Testament is a Sacrament, long before the name was given to any document
    - It is not until 190 AD that we begin to find that phrase "the New Testament" used
  to describe a book a document
    - It is not until the late 4th century that the Church definitively ratified the
    canonical form of that book that we now call the New Testasment
    - By then the New Testament sacrifice had already been evangelized the whole world.
    - The book is called New Testament because of its proximity to the sacrifice of the Eucharist
    which Jesus called the New Testament
  - The Scriptures were originally liturgical documents and they need to be read as such
  on their own terms in order to be properly understood
- What did the first christians mean by "New Testament"
  - The phrase appears just six times in the "New Testament" books that were eventually
  brought together as the biblical New Testament
  - Since there was no books at that time, Jesus and his early followers must have
  meant something else:
    - Testament = Diatheke (G) = Berith (H) = more precisely "Covenant" in English
    - The legal means by which sacred kinship was extended to an individual or to a group
    to the Jews of Jesus' time, a covenant is what created a family bond where none had
    existed before
    - A covenant is marked by a solemn ritual, so important that is served as a
    shorthand terms for their personal and collective identity as family
      - E.g.: Saint Paul referring to his fellow Jews as simply "the circumcision", because
      the ritual of circumcision was the sign of the old covenant
    - Covenant also refers complexity of social relations, laws, and instituitions
      - The law of Moses = 'the book of covenant'
    - The term "New Covenant"
      - from the Book of Old Testasment: 1 time "the days are coming when I will make a new
      covenant with the house of Israel..." (Jer 31:31-34)
      - Jeremiah's prophecise indicating a new convenant in continuity with the old:
        - like to old in that would involve law, kinship and blessings
        - unlike the old because it would be everlasting, remain unbroken and interior
      - (reminded) evokes Moses' ratification on Israel's covenant with God on Mt Sinai
    - Jesus uses only 1, but not to describe a book but a sacramental bond
    - Saint Paul presents the earliest historical record of the evant, over 20 years after the fact
      - "the Lord Jesus on the night when was betrayed took bread... " (1 Conrin 11:23-26)
    - This event appears in all synoptic Gospels, Matthew, Mark, Luke
- In Jesus' only use of the term "New Covenant", we find that New Testament refers not to a text,
but to a rite and to the new order brought about by that rite
  - Use of the term in the Epistles confirms this association
  - The term "new testament" appears consistently in discussion of liturgy, divine worship
  priestly service
  - "God have made the Apostles competent to be ministers of a new convenant" (2Corin),
  not authors
    - (sacred )minister = diakonoi (G) = deacons = the title of a liturgical office
  - 2 reference of the term new covenant amid a discussion of the transfer of the sacrificial
  priesthood of the Levites of the old to the new (early in letters to Hebrews)
  - "Christ as the mediator of a new covenant. He is the mediating priest whose prinkled
  blood speaks more graciously than the blood of Abel" (later in letters to Hebrews, chap12?),
  referenced to the Church's liturgy, called the assembly, or church of the first-born. The letter
  also tells the assemly has gathered at mount Zion, the site where Jesus celebrated the Last Supp.
  - So when the phrase "New Covenant" appears in the documents we now know as the "New Testament",
  it appears consistently amid discussions of sacrificial liturgy and priestly office, divine
  worship. The covenant is equated with its ritual sign and is administered by men who hold
  sacrificial office
- So where did "Testament" come from (instead of Covenant)?
  - the distinction appears only in Western translations: in the original Greek or Syriac, Coptic,
  and Armenian, there is no distinction.
  - the diatheke (G) and berith (H) presents a problem for the first Latin translators, they
  found no exact equivalent in Latin so they settled on the Latin term Testamentum, which is often
  associated with bequests - "my last will and testament", imprecise and inadequate
    - in the culture extended from Jeremiah to Jesus, a covenant accomplishes more than a testament
    could ever do: a covenent was the the bond that constituted Israel's life, law, and liturgy
- The deepest meaning of New Testament:
  - For Jesus and the Apostles, the "New Testament" was not a book, not words that were written down
  it was the Mass. And in the text of the New Testament, the phrase "New Testament" denotes not a
  text but an action, not a document but a sacrament.
  - Long before the New Testament was a document then, it was  a sacrament, established by Jesus
  and entrusted to His Church.
  - The Sacrament is prior to the document, according to the very words of the document

- **The Mass is the New Testament**

### The Eucharist Foreshadowed: Typology

- Typology discerns in God's works of the Old Covenant prefigurations of what he
accomplished in the fullness of time
  - The lesson: all of scripture, or history, is one single story. From the beginning
  God's story already anticipate and prepare its glorious end, the finale, climax.
- Jesus walking with the disciples in the road to Emmaus (Luke, 24:27): beginning
with Moses and the prophets, He intepreted to them in all the scriptures the things concerning
himself.
  - "That everything written about me in the law of Moses and the prophets and the psalms must be
  fulfilled"
- The **TYPE**: Saint Paul "Adam was a type of the one who was to come", Adam prefigured Jesus
because he was beloved by God, because he was tried and tested, and because he was the firstborn
of many brethren in the human family. Saint Paul talk this way about Noah's ark, a type of baptism
  - Typos (G) (1 Peter 3:18-22): being put to death in the flesh but made alive in the spirit
  - baptism not as a removal of dirt from the body but as an appeal to God for a clear conscience
- One story, one narrative that unites the old and new testament:
- Christ is presented by evangelists and the apostles as
  - new Adam, Moses, son of David (Solomon)
- Saint Augustine: "The new testament is concealed in the old and the old is revealed in the new"
- The Mass is foreshadowed throughout the Old testament:
  - Genesis presents Adam as a priest, presiding over the garden santuary, guards and works the garden, just as a priest would guard and minister the tabernacle an the temple
  - Abel, Noah, Abraham functioned as priests in their time: offering sacrifices, stood as mediators between God and humanity
  - Melchizedek (Gen 14) is the first character in the Bible is identified by the word "priest", or "cohen" (H), priest-king of Salem, offering very unusual sacrifice, offering bread and wine
  - This is the same elements that Jesus would use in his own unusual sacrifice. Presented later in the book of Hebrew "in the line of Melchizedek", thus Melchizedek is the type of Jesus, the heavenly high priest, and his offering is the type of the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass
- Holy Mass as Hidden Manna:
  - the passover meal itself, unleavened bread, cups of wine, and the presence of the Lamb of God: Jesus is identified as "our Passover, our pascal lamb, who has been sacrifice" (saint Paul)
  - Feeding of the people in the wilderness, Jesus cited that Manna is a type of his bread (John 6)
  - by the book Revelation, Exodus 24:8 "Behold the blood of the convent which the Lord has made with you" 
  - burn offering = 'ola and hattat' (before the kingdom of David)
  - during David's: TO.DAH: the thank offering, a sacrifice of bread and wine, usually offered by an individual in thanksgiving for deliverance from some grave peril. The worshiper often sang a "new song" to the Lord
  - Philo of Alexandria (Jewish philosopher, and biblical commentator, 1st century) wrote in Greek about the todah, and translated with the Greek word "eucharistia", which is so central in to biblical religion after King David: "in the coming messianic age, all sacrifices will cease except the todah sacrifice, this will never cease in all eternity" = the clearest type
  - Tabernacle of Moses and Temple of Solomon: **bread** of the **presences** = showbread = bread was brought before God and consumed by the priests, brought in on a marble table and afterward kept on a gold table in recognition that it had been changed in the sanctuary by the very presence of God
  - The Mishna (ancient Jewish documents): "the priest used to lift the golden table up and exhibit the bread of the presence on it to those who came up for the festivals saying to them, "Behold God's love for you". In time, the type would be fulfilled: Jesus makes it available to all who shared the common priesthood thru christian baptism
  - King Solomon spoke of the banquet where divine wisdom would welcome everyone: "come eat of my bread and drink of the wine I have mixed" (Proverb 9:1-5), "for wisdom has filled her house, she has setup her seven pillars"

  - Prophet Elijah (1Kings 19:5-8) who was fed loaves of heavenly bread by the hands of angels

## Lesson 5: the Mass in the Gospel

- Typology: the Church, as early as apostolic times, and then constantly in her Tradition, has
illuminated the unity of the divine plan in the two Testaments through typology, which discerns in
God's works of the Old Covenant prefigurations of what He accomplished in the fullness of time in
the person of His incarnate Son (CCC, p128)

- Climax of Jesus' life was anticipated from the beginning of the Gospel.
  - the early Chruch Fathers: bloodshed of circumcision anticipate is a type that would be fulfilled
  in his suffering on the cross, a Magi's myrrh anticipates Jesus' annointing for burial, the
  swaddling bands are a foreshadowing of his burial cloths
- Pattern through out the course of Jesus' ministry
  - Jesus' birthplace is Bethlehem, significant place because of King David's birthplace and for
  its name Bethlehem (H) = house of Bread (Jesus idenfitieds himself as the Bread of Life).
  - His parents placed him not in a scrib but a manger, a feeding trough, a place where animals
  came to consume their grain
  - The most celebrated miracles of Jesus prefigures the Eucharist:
    - Cana's wedding feast, Jesus changes one substances to the other, water to wine,
    and then at the Last Supper, wine to blood.
    - Multiplying of bread and fish (Matthew 14:14-20)
    - Taking, blessing, breaking, sharing, the same sequence of actions as in the Last Supper
    - The 10 banquets of Luke: 
      - at the house of Levi the tax collector (Lk 5:27-32),
      - at the house of Simon the Pharisee (Lk 7:36-50),
      - hospitality of Mary, Martha, and Lazarus (Lk 10:38-42),
      - a Pharisee's house (Lk 11:37-52)
      - Zacchaeus the tax collector (Lk 19:1-10)
    - solemn banquets as typical setting for Jesus' teaching and his fellowship with disciples, types
    that find fulfillment at the Last Supper
  - Saint John's Theology of the Eucharistic presence, explained by Jesus
    - The woman at the well wants water to satisfy her thrist forever
      - Jesus pointed forward to the water at baptism (John 4:23-24)
    - The crowd at Carpernaum wanted bread that would feed them forever
      - Jesus pointed forward to the Mass (John 6:24-34) "Lord give us this bread always"
    - "Body", "Flesh", "Blood" means literally His and shock the Jews. He uses graphic languages:
    His verbs are more graphic in the Greek: he tells them to "chew" or "gnaw" his flesh. Yet when the people express their disgust, Jesus continues to insist on the graphic and realistic language rather than reverting to the generic term "eat" that might have been taken figuratively.
      - it does seem outlandish and weird, causing later Roman persecutors to accuse Christians of cannibalism
      - these are unequivocal words of the New Testament: the bread of life discourse is one of the the longest teachings of Jesus that have been preserved in the Gospels, and it focused on the eternal life that Jesus came to give the world through both faith and eating and drinking.
    - Jesus' words at the last supper is precise as they were at the Bread of Life discourse, using bread and wine which would have been elements in any solemn Jewish meal
  - The instituition narrative (all 3 synoptic Gospels), corresponding exactly to John's the Bread ofLife discourse:
    - Jesus and the Apostles shared three of the fourth cups prescribed in the seder ritual
    - Jesus followed the established liturgy for almost all of the Passover meal, then omitted the fourth cup, and even drew attention to this omission, he said "from now I shall not drink of the fruit of the vine until the kingdom of God comes" (Luke 22:18), then at the end of the meal he led his Apostles out into the night, singing the Hallel psalm, even though the hadn't consumed the fourth and final cup of wine.
    - This omission makes no sense unless we see Our Lord suffering on the cross, because there he
    is offered wine on a sponge, which he accepts, and that when he says "It it finished",
    consummated, accomplished.
    - There on the Cross, Jesus drank the fourth cup and completed what he had begun in the Upper Room. Our salvation is complete, once for all, from the moment forwad. But Jesus wanted it to be applied, represented, in every generation. That's precisely why He instituted the Eucharist, and gave His chosen Twelve the command to "do this" in remembrance of him
  - In case his command wasn't clear enough, he would later model the behavior he expected from them,
  on the Road to Emmaus (Luke 24), even the disciple heard the annoucement of His Ressurection, they  believe him to be dead:
    - Jesus pointed out to them "that the Christ should suffer and then enter into his glory", then he gave them the typological reading of the whole Bible as we read "Beginning with Moses and all of the prophets," he interpreted to them, in ALL the scriptures the things concerning himself, that must have been an amazing Bible study. A tour de force.
    - But they still didn't recognize him, so when they arrived at their destination, he sat down at
    the table with them, and "take, bless, break, share" the bread, and THEN they recognized him.
    - That scene shows the very structure of the Mass we still celebrated today. Jesus opens up the 
    Scriptures for us in the liturgy of the word, and he breaks the bread for us in the liturgy of
    the Eucharist
  - Saint John's theology of the Eucharistic Presence:
    - after the resurrection, Jesus waiting at the shore with bread and fish
    - Graydon Snyder (Baptist Scripture scholar) recognizes that this is clearly a type of the
    Holy Eucharist
    - Fish = common symbol of early Christians of the sacrament, in art and in literature
    - There is a growing awareness, among both Catholic and Protestant Biblical scholars
    that the Fourth Gospels were composed to be read among Christians gathered for worship,
    especially on the Lord's Day. The evangeists set them down on paper so that they could be easily proclaimed in the liturgy. And so they wrote them with the very keen sensitivity to the Holy Eucharist. It would forever be the context of the proclamation of the Gospels. So they made it a great part of the content of their Gospels as well.


## Lesson 6: the Mass in Acts and the New Testament letters

- We've seen the fulfillment of the Gospel narratives, but the decisive scriptural foundation of
our faith in the Holy Eucharist is actually in the rest of the books of the New Testament.
- These books are so important because they show us how the Christians, the disciples of the Lord,
fulfilled the command of the Lord Jesus
- What did the disciples do? We see their follow-through in the opening chapters of the Acts 
  - On Pentecost the Holy Spirit comes to them in power and the Church's rituals and offices
  begin to fall right into place
  - We find Peter and the other Apostles presiding over a church that is profoundly sacramental.
  - From day 1, the Church practices baptism, and from day one, the Church meets in fellowship,
  and practices another ritual
  - Acts 2 mentions it twice, telling us that they disciples met in house churches to berak bread (Acts 2:41-42)
- 4 defining activities of the primitive church
  - Apostles' teaching
  - Fellowship (Koinonia (G) = Communion)
  - Breaking of Bread
  - Prayers
- Where did they come to us as the 21st century Christian and where did they come together for the early Fathers in the Church?
  - Saint Justin Martyr (wrote in 150): he'll tell you how the churches in Italy met every Sunday and read the memoirs of the Apostles, how they prayed a Eucharistic prayer and broke bread for communion, Justin's account of the Mass is so detailed that the Catechism of the Catholic Church includes it verbatim as its description of "The Mass of All Ages"
    - On the day we call the day of the sun, all who dwell in the city or country gather in the same place. The momoirs of the apostles and the writings of the prophets are read as much as time permits. When the reader has finished he who presides over those gathered admonishes and challenges them to imitate these beautiful things. Then we all rise together and offer prayers for ourselves and for all others, wherever they may be. So that we may be found righteous by our life and actions, and faithful to the commandments, so as to obtain eternal salvation. When the prayers are concluded, we exchange the kiss. Then someone brings bread and a cup of water and wine mixed together to him who presides over the breathren. He takes them and offers praise and glory to the Father of the universe, through the name of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and for a considerable time he gives thanks (eucharistian (G)) that we may be judged worthy of these gifts. When he concludes the prayers of thanksgiving, all present give voice to an acclamation by saying: "Amen". When he who presides has given thanks and the people have responded, those whom we call deacons give to those present the 'eucharisted' bread, wine and water and take them to those who are absent.

  - Saint Paul was concerned about what was happening at the liturgical table in Rome, Corith and Galatia. He heard that some Christians were excluding entire classes of other: rich Christians excluding the poor, Jews were making Gentiles feel unwelcome. This was not the spirit Jesus intended for the Church, or the liturgy and it's not what Paul had taught the churches he founded.

All of these rituals are sacrifical, but the Eucharist stands apart as the only valid offering in the age of the new Covenant (1Corin 10:21). By this time, St. Paul has firmly established the sacred solemnity of the Christian banquet, and how it's quite different from pagan sacrifices. It's different even from the offerings in the Jerusalem Tempe, which were animal sacrifices. In the Eucharist, God is truly present under the appearances of bread and wine, and when beleivers consume these elements, they enter in the communion with the Almighty.

## Lesson 7: the Mass as Heaven on Earth: Hebrews and Revelation

- Both books are rich in ritual liturgy, talking about sacrifice and alters, priests, and banquets
- Hebrews 12:22-24, 13:10: where else do we worship with the angels and saints and approach an altar, and receive the blood of the Lord, sounds a lot like the Mass.
- John the Seer in the book of Revelation: altars with lamp stands, priests wearing vestments, and an altar, and readings from a holy book, along with chalices and incense (Rev 8:3-4)
- Hebrews and Revelation are liturgical pillars of a Catholic worldview
  - Father A.G. Martimort (20th Catholic theologians): "these two books do for the church what the
  vision on Mount Sinai did for Moses"
  - establishes the model, the standard, a paragon which provides a parttern for Christian worship for the rest of history. The content of both books is profoundly liturgical
- Early Church Father were scriptual in their approach to the liturgy and liturgical in their reading of Scripture. They were Catholic in reading the scripture
- Recent Commentators? Not so accommodating
  - By denying sacrifical value, rejecting entirely the Church's theology of the sacrament, even Eucharist, it is struggling to understand Hebrews and Rev.
  - Luther even suggested to remove Rev. from the Bible perhaps its dense sacrificial imagery didn't fit his new view of worship.
  - Evangelical scholars F.F. Bruce and Ronald Williamson have argued that Hebrews is non and anti-litrgical  "True religion or the worship of God is not tied to externalities of any kind" (FF. Bruce)
  - Bruce recognizes however, that the letter of the Hebrews is preoccupied with the idea of covenant, and as we know covenant is the key to understanding the whole Sacred Scripture
- Hebrews compares and contrasts the old and new covenant: including high priest, performing ministry in a sanctuary, enterining into a Holy Place to offer the bood of sacrifices. The blood brings about purification and redemption for worshipers who have sinned. The mediation of both covenants is therefore primarily liturgical.
  - Letter to the Hebrews uses the word covenant more than all the other books of the New Testament combined (17/33 occurrences). H uses "new covenantl" which appears in only one other place (by Jesus)
  - Jesus expicitly ratified the New Covenant by instituting the Eucharist
- What is Hebrews trying to tel us by its repeated use of the language of covenant and priesthood and bood. Covenant and Priesthood:
  - Jesus sacrifice does not consists simply in his suffering and death on the cross but more precisely in his perfect of self-offering to God. His suffering and death are thus terminated definitively, at Calvary, but not his priestly self-offering, which he continues forever in heaven, precisely in his deified humanity, which was curcified, then ressurected, ascended, but is still now enthroned "at the right hand" of the Father (another phrase we hear repeated throughout Hebrews)
  - Christ' crucified and glorified humanity thus embodies the new convenant in several ways: 
    - His is the body of our heavenly high priest
    - His body is our sanctuary
    - His body is the sacrifice of the new covenant which constitutes the everlasting liturgy in the heavenly Jerusalem
    - This heavenly liturgy is what the church on earth enters by the Power of the holy spirit through the Eucharist: you have (NOT will) come to the heavenly Jerusalem and to Jesus the mediator of a new covenant
  - Hebrews thus, echoes the gospels in proclaiming how Jesus made, through his death, both a perfect sacrifice and everlasting liturgical action, the Mass, the blessed sacrament

- The book of revelation has the liturgy at its core:
  - we see that everlasting liturgy in its eternal and celestial dimensions
  - we see thru John's vision Jesus' offering from a heaveny perspective, angels' and saints' points of view
  - many layers of meaning to Revelation, but deepest meaning is not as weird and esoteric as some people would lead us to believe.
  - its core meaning has nothing to do with millennial hysteria, the Rapture, or the Antichrist
  - its core meaning is the liturgy of heaven, in which we participate in the Mass
  - Evidences:
    - in the structure: the book devided neatly into 2 halves:
      - first begins with repentance and culminating in the opening of the scrolls and their proclamation
      - second begins with the opening of the heavenly temple culminating in the marriage supper of the lamb
    - 2-fold structure of the Mass
    - Sunday worship, the Lord's day, altars with lamp stands, candles, incense, priests consecrated celibates wearing vestments, and an altar, and readings from a holy book, along with the Eucharistic Host and the chalices (Rev 8:3-4)
    - familiar prayers (in part or in full): the Sanctus, the Gloria, the Alleluia, sign of the cross
    - the golden threads of the liturgy is what holds together the apocalyptic pearls of John's reveation from the beginning through the middle, to the end (Rev 4:8)
  - The liturgical understanding of Rev is pervasive in the ancients Fathers and it is still the common reading in many Eastern Churches where the Apocalyse is actually referred to as , an "icon of the liturgy"
  - Christ is invoked 4 times as the "Lamb of God" in the Mass, 28 times in 22 chapters of Rev, more
  than any other titles.
  - The marriage feast of the lamb is the climax of the Apocalypse (Rev 19:9)
  - why would Saint John hide what was hidden already?, take what is hidden and make it even more obscure
    - Revelation isn't a theological work as much as it is a visionary reflection
    - with destruction of Jerusalem in 70AD, genuinely trauma, 
      - Marking covenant by: circumcision (Israel Old Cov) vs. baptism (New Cov)
      - Day of rest and worship: sabbath (Old Cov) vs. the Lord's day (New Cov)
    - Jesus did not merely a conclude the Old Conv but a fulfill it by investing the trappings of the Old Covenant with even greater glory. E.g. no longer a central sanctuary on earth made by hands, because now Rev shows Christ the King is enthroned in the heavely Jerusalem, presisely where he acts as the high priest in the Holy of Holies
    - Does that means the Church can't have a building, candles and incense, Rev clearly answers that we can have all of these and heaven, too
- In Hebrews and Rev. the Mass is the message: you can't get the message if you don't know the Mass, without the Mass, all you're left with in these books is a series of rather strange and disconnected images. If you and I want to make sense of these books we need to learn to read them just as the first Christians did. When we look into these matters with the new eyes of new covenant faith, we'll see the sense amid the strangeness and we'll see the biblical glory hidden in the mundane in next Sunday's Mass
