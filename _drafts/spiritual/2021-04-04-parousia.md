## Lesson 1: biblical worship is Ritual and Sacraficial

- The Holy Sacrifice of the Mass is everywhere in the bible
  - The bible is all about the Mass, found by all church fathers
  - Relationship between Sacred scripture and devine worship: the bible and the
  Mass is made for each other:
    - The sacred scripture describes and prescribes the ritual of God's peopleo
    - The bible gives us the content and context of the liturgy
    - So we cannot read the bible as it own but from the heart of the church, the heart of Jesus
- What is Liturgy:
  - from greek word meaning public service/worshipk
  - in the bible: ritual public worship observed by the people of God
  - Protestant: liturgy is something negative, rote, mechanical, unthinking
    - worship should be spontaneous and free, because Jesus came to liberate us
    from the ancient work of the Law
  - is there from the beginning of the Bible, redirecting all efforts to worship God
    - work was ordered to worship, labor to liturgy
    - the cosmos is the santuary of God's presence
    = Israel's tabernacle built in Exodus
      - built in 6 days, consecrated on the 7th
    - Eden = temple of Jerusalem
      - built on mountain top, over stream of living water
    - santuary is entered by a gate, guarded by Cherumbims
  - the world = temple
    - adam = high priest
    - garden of eden = santuary
    - ("till and keep") 'abad and shamar (H) expresses humanity's spritual vocation to worship
    (Number 18:7) = minister and guard the holiness of God
    - Adam committed the original sin is not a transgression but desecration
  - in Eden, sacrifice means something simple from the earth and of human hands
  - after the fall, it becomes bloody
    - Cain from harvest and Abel from livestock
    - Since Abel's sacrifice is more pleasing, Cain murdered/sacrificed Abel
  - the point of the story:
    - not scacrific and ritual are bad
    - sacrifice and ritual should be outward expression of an interior worship of the heart
    that is pure, it is mercy that God desires, not the mechanics of the scacrifice
  - Liturgy is the province of the Partriarchs in the families
    - Father offers sacrifice from family
    - The patriarchs are priests
    - Liturgy is central to the religion they observe in their own families
  -  Sacred Liturgy and the Rituals of Sacrifices, the Passover
    - from the male lamb, painting of the doorposts, communal sacrificial meals
    - passing the red sea = religious procession, a pigrimage
    - "more than half of the Pentateuch, the most authoritative section of the
    Old Testaments, consists of ritual regulations" (Protestant scholar Gordon Wenham)
  - **God gets nothing out of worhip, but WE DO**
  - God's law is far more liturgical than it is ethical in its content
    - The entirely of the law can be seen simply as etiquette for meals shared
    in the presence of God, and those meals are ritual, sacrificial, and priestly
  - The Jerusalem Temple the place of sacrifice
    - Valid sacrifice can take place nowhere else on earth
    - There can be no other temple than the one established by God, through the
    King he has designated as his son, the son of David, the son of God
    - Sacrifice is offered for the atonement for the sins of Israels
  - The Lord displeases with the sacrifices of Israels, not because of the liturgy
  or the sacrifices, but because of the humans' hearts
    - Christ observes the rituals of Israels: pay temple tax, go to the temple,
    synagogue, he doesn't abolish the liturgy, not altogether, but establish
    the new one more powerful.
  - The early church takes its cues from Jesus, leading liturgical life:
    - Acts: the Apostles observing rituals: baptism, breaking of the Eucharistic bread,
    anointing the people, laying on of hands, absolving sins, ritual meals
    - Romans: is like Moses' laws in this sense, containing etiquettes for meals,
    shared in the presense of God
  - The Liturgy of the Apostles is Sacrificial
    - Letter Hebrews + Revelation describe the people is royal and priestly, and
    its rituals as offereings, focusing on altars, prayers speaking of atonement
    brought about by blood, the blood of Jesus
  - The Old and New testament is written down for the sake of these rites, the rituals
  of divine worship.
    - The Bible content is liturgical and the liturgy is the context in which the 
    scriptures were written.
