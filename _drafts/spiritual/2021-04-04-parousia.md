## Lesson 1: biblical worship is Ritual and Sacraficial

    - The bible gives us the content and context of the liturgy
    - So we cannot read the bible as it own but from the heart of the church, the heart of Jesus
- What is Liturgy:
  - from greek word meaning public service/worshipk
  - in the bible: ritual public worship observed by the people of God
  - Protestant: liturgy is something negative, rote, mechanical, unthinking
    - worship should be spontaneous and free, because Jesus came to liberate us
    from the ancient work of the Law
  - is there from the beginning of the Bible, redirecting all efforts to worship God
    - work was ordered to worship, labor to liturgy
    - the cosmos is the santuary of God's presence
    = Israel's tabernacle built in Exodus
      - built in 6 days, consecrated on the 7th
    - Eden = temple of Jerusalem
      - built on mountain top, over stream of living water
    - santuary is entered by a gate, guarded by Cherumbims
  - the world = temple
    - adam = high priest
    - garden of eden = santuary
    - ("till and keep") 'abad and shamar (H) expresses humanity's spritual vocation to worship
    (Number 18:7) = minister and guard the holiness of God
    - Adam committed the original sin is not a transgression but desecration
  - in Eden, sacrifice means something simple from the earth and of human hands
  - after the fall, it becomes bloody
    - Cain from harvest and Abel from livestock
    - Since Abel's sacrifice is more pleasing, Cain murdered/sacrificed Abel
  - the point of the story:
    - not scacrific and ritual are bad

    - passing the red sea = religious procession, a pigrimage

    Old Testaments, consists of ritual regulations" (Protestant scholar Gordon Wenham)
  - **God gets nothing out of worhip, but WE DO**
  - God's law is far more liturgical than it is ethical in its content
    - The entirely of the law can be seen simply as etiquette for meals shared

  - The Liturgy of the Apostles is Sacrificial
    - Letter Hebrews + Revelation describe the people is royal and priestly, and
    its rituals as offereings, focusing on altars, prayers speaking of atonement

  - The Old and New testament is written down for the sake of these rites, the rituals

    - The Bible content is liturgical and the liturgy is the context in which the

## Lesson 2: the Covenant
- the idea at the foundation of the Bible, the bond that makes us the family of God on earth
  - covenant = testament = diatheke (G) = berith (H)
- what is a covenant
  - an agreement that establishes or restores a family bond where none had previously
  existed or one had been broken: marriage, adoption
  - not the same as contract
    - covenant: exchange persons ("I am yours and you are mine")
    - contract: exchange goods or services ("This is yours and this is mine")
    - covenant includes a contract, because covenant involves obligations towards another
    - covenant is sealed by a solemn oath, not just a promise or signature on a piece of paper
      - God is called to witness and to enforce the terms of the relationship
      - lifelong and unbreakable
  - terms of covenant:
    - swearing an oath
    - declaring the terms of relationship
    - signing the agreement
    - establishes a sign of the new relationship
- covenant of creation, a covenant with creatures:
  - God has done this with humanity, making them not merely servants but sons and daughters
  - God fulfills all the ancient requirement of a covenant
    - Adam: sign = the Sabbath
    - Noah: sign = rainbow
    - Abraham: sign = circumcision
    - Israel: sign = Passover
    - David and his lineage: sign = kingship
  - Each is the renewal of the first covenant:
    - covenant of creation, of marriage of adam and eve: a family bond between God and his creatures
    - God first gave kinship to married couple, Adam and Eve
    - Extending to a household, Noah
    - Then the tribe of Abraham,
    - Then the nation of Israel, 12 tribes
    - Finally to the kingdom of David
  - Each new covenant came with renewed law and renewed order or worship
    - profound effect on the liturgy, Biblica region is covenantal
    - God seals and renews all the biblical covenants by liturgical signs
  - The purpose of covenants:
    - All the covenant prefigur and foreshadow a covenant the would be definitive and new and eternal
    - This covenant is sealed not by animals' blood by the blood of the Son of God
    - It is so important, hence Jesus uses it with maximum effect, only once at the Last Supper
  - The passover meal:
    - The meal that seals God's covenant with Moses, fulfilling liturgical command of the Old Conv.
    - Also changing it by his own divine authority: this passover is different from all those
    that had gone before
    - This cup which is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood
      - The son of God is establishing a new covenant, offering his own body and blood in
      the appearances of bread and wine
    - the Bread of Life discourse, in an early passover (a year or 2 before)
      - Synagogue in Capernaum
      - John 6:49-58, invoke the Old Covenant and contrast it with the sign of the new covenant
      - to be in covenant with God, then is to have life in its fullness, to live forever in God's
      family and to share his table
      - to consume the bread of life is more than just to have something more than a relationship
      with God, but to have communion with him, to be united with him
      - 2 Peter 1:4 it is to become partakers of the divine nature
      - The Jews was shocked and many of his disciples backed down and no longer followed him
      - But Jesus never hedges his statement or qualified his terms, or said, if it was
      only symbolically or metaphorically. These are the non-negotiable terms of the New Covenant
    - The mandatory sign of the covenant was the sharing of Jesus' body and blood under
    the appearances of bread and wine
    - This ritual which Jesus instiuted at the Last Supper was to be re-enacted throughout the
    rest of history
    - "Do this in memory of me": memory is an essential part of every covenant renewal
      - a memorical sacrifice = a remembrance of the terms of the covenant, a reminder of Israel's
      identity as the family of God
      - The Jews didn't think of memory the way we do today: not the purely psychological act
      of recalling the past, not nostalgia
      - It was about making the past present, again and again
    - E.g. Passover as Exodus
      - renewed and direct experience of the Exodus
      - the children of Israel of every generation were expected to participate
      in their national's primordial libration (they have to travel to Jerusalem)
    - This new ritual established by the Lord would be the fulfillment of all the
    rituals of the Old Covenant
      - Jesus's sacrifice rendered Israel's previous ceremonial laws obsolete like
      prophecies that were fulfilled
    - It does not, however, dispensed entirely with ritual worship
      - All Israel's traditional worship were like mighty rivers that flowed into the
      infite ocean of adoration that Jesus established for the Church, the Divine
      Worship of the New Covenant: they didn't vanish there, the found completion;
      they weren't abolished, they were fulfilled
      - All the older covenants were recapitualated by Jesus in the New
  - From the covenant to Communion:
    - Saint Paul: all the language of convenant in the Old Testament gives way to the language
    of Communion
    - the Mass : Koinonia (G) = Communion or Participation
    - Communion in the blood of Christ: "the cup of blessing which we bless is it not
    a participation in the blood of Christ" (1 Corin 10:16)
    - Becase there is one Bread, we who are many are one body for we all partake in the one bread
  - Shattered boundaries
    - Saint Paul makes explitcit what was implicit in Jesus' Bread of Life Discourse
    - The Lord's salvation has shattered not only the boundaries between Israel and the nations
    but also between God and the World
    - Communion is now possilbe between God and humanity, also among all peoples, both Jews and
    Gentiles
    - God's family is now, at long last, universal, Catholic
- The new Covenant
  - is indeed something new and ternal, great, and glorious
  - but it also the continuity of the past
  - **Israel's ritual worship was ordered to covenant remembrance which took place
  in reading, and a covenant renewal which took place in sacrifice**
  - Christian worship, also, is a remembrance of God's mighty works in history
  through Jesus' saving passion, ressurection and glorification
  - The Christian Eucharist remains both a covenant renewal and a thanksgiving for
  God's continued presences among his people
  - That presence, **PAROUSIA** is a true communion, Christ comes to abide in us and we
  come to abide in him
  - We thus, become divinized or deified, become sons in the Son, "the children of God"
  throught the communion with the only Son of God
- All the language of Sacred Scripture is covenantal and all the signs of the liturgy are convenantal
  - We can't understand the Bible, the Old or the New apart from the convenantal liturgy
  - We can't understand the Mass, without an understanding of the biblical notion of covenant.


## Lesson 3: the New Testament: document or sacrament

- Where in the Bible do you find the sacrifice of the Mass
  - Calvary is a sacrifice alright, but the Mass is just a meal
- Jesus's sacrifice on the cross is one and for all and there is no saving sacrifice
apart from the cross, but there is nothing about what happened on Calvary that would have
suggested sacrifice to any first-centural Jews
  - No devout Jews witnessing Jesus' crucifixion on Good Friday would have gone home
  and said that they had just witnessed a sacrifice
  - For Jews, a sacrifice must:
    - take place inside the Temple
    - on top of an altar
    - with a Levitical priest presiding
  - Jesus' crucifixion:
    - took place outside the walls,
    - no Temple, no altar, no priest
    - look nothing more than a bloody Roman execution
- From Execution to Sacrifice
  - Transformative answer = Jesus' offering of his body and blood at the Last Supper
  - Because Jesus spoke of that offering in sacrifical terms:
    - he called it the New Covenant in his Blood, echoiing the words of Moses,
    - Moses had ratified the old covenant at Mt. Sinai with a sacrifice:
    - "Behold the blood of the convenant which the Lord has made with you" (Exodus)
  - The apostles in turn looked upon Jesus' memorial in liturgical and sacrificial terms
  - "For Christ, our paschal lamb has been sacrificed" (1Con5:7)
  - So Holy Thursday is what transformed Good Friday from an execution into a Sacrifice
  - Easter Sunday is what transformed that sacrifice into the Blessed Sacrament
    - Because now Christ's Body is raised in glory, and by the power of the Holy Spirit,
    it's communicable to the faithful
    - It is the same sacrifice that Jesus offered when he instituted the Eucharist
    and then dying on Calvary, only now, his sacred humanity is glorified deified and deifying to us
    - It is the high-priestly sacrifice that he offers in heaven and on earth
    - That is the holy sacrifice of the Mass.
- If the Eucharist were only a meal, then Calvary would only be an execution
- SO where in the New Testament is the Sacrifice of the Mass
  - **The Sacrifice of the Mass is the New Testament**
  - The New Testament is a Sacrament, long before the name was given to any document
    - It is not until 190 AD that we begin to find that phrase "the New Testament" used
  to describe a book a document
    - It is not until the late 4th century that the Church definitively ratified the
    canonical form of that book that we now call the New Testasment
    - By then the New Testament sacrifice had already been evangelized the whole world.
    - The book is called New Testament because of its proximity to the sacrifice of the Eucharist
    which Jesus called the New Testament
  - The Scriptures were originally liturgical documents and they need to be read as such
  on their own terms in order to be properly understood
- What did the first christians mean by "New Testament"
  - The phrase appears just six times in the "New Testament" books that were eventually
  brought together as the biblical New Testament
  - Since there was no books at that time, Jesus and his early followers must have
  meant something else:
    - Testament = Diatheke (G) = Berith (H) = more precisely "Covenant" in English
    - The legal means by which sacred kinship was extended to an individual or to a group
    to the Jews of Jesus' time, a covenant is what created a family bond where none had
    existed before
    - A covenant is marked by a solemn ritual, so important that is served as a
    shorthand terms for their personal and collective identity as family
      - E.g.: Saint Paul referring to his fellow Jews as simply "the circumcision", because
      the ritual of circumcision was the sign of the old covenant
    - Covenant also refers complexity of social relations, laws, and instituitions
      - The law of Moses = 'the book of covenant'
    - The term "New Covenant"
      - from the Book of Old Testasment: 1 time "the days are coming when I will make a new
      covenant with the house of Israel..." (Jer 31:31-34)
      - Jeremiah's prophecise indicating a new convenant in continuity with the old:
        - like to old in that would involve law, kinship and blessings
        - unlike the old because it would be everlasting, remain unbroken and interior
      - (reminded) evokes Moses' ratification on Israel's covenant with God on Mt Sinai
    - Jesus uses only 1, but not to describe a book but a sacramental bond
    - Saint Paul presents the earliest historical record of the evant, over 20 years after the fact
      - "the Lord Jesus on the night when was betrayed took bread... " (1 Conrin 11:23-26)
    - This event appears in all synoptic Gospels, Matthew, Mark, Luke
- In Jesus' only use of the term "New Covenant", we find that New Testament refers not to a text,
but to a rite and to the new order brought about by that rite
  - Use of the term in the Epistles confirms this association
  - The term "new testament" appears consistently in discussion of liturgy, divine worship
  priestly service
  - "God have made the Apostles competent to be ministers of a new convenant" (2Corin),
  not authors
    - (sacred )minister = diakonoi (G) = deacons = the title of a liturgical office
  - 2 reference of the term new covenant amid a discussion of the transfer of the sacrificial
  priesthood of the Levites of the old to the new (early in letters to Hebrews)
  - "Christ as the mediator of a new covenant. He is the mediating priest whose prinkled
  blood speaks more graciously than the blood of Abel" (later in letters to Hebrews, chap12?),
  referenced to the Church's liturgy, called the assembly, or church of the first-born. The letter
  also tells the assemly has gathered at mount Zion, the site where Jesus celebrated the Last Supp.
  - So when the phrase "New Covenant" appears in the documents we now know as the "New Testament",
  it appears consistently amid discussions of sacrificial liturgy and priestly office, divine
  worship. The covenant is equated with its ritual sign and is administered by men who hold
  sacrificial office
- So where did "Testament" come from (instead of Covenant)?
  - the distinction appears only in Western translations: in the original Greek or Syriac, Coptic,
  and Armenian, there is no distinction.
  - the diatheke (G) and berith (H) presents a problem for the first Latin translators, they
  found no exact equivalent in Latin so they settled on the Latin term Testamentum, which is often
  associated with bequests - "my last will and testament", imprecise and inadequate
    - in the culture extended from Jeremiah to Jesus, a covenant accomplishes more than a testament
    could ever do: a covenent was the the bond that constituted Israel's life, law, and liturgy
- The deepest meaning of New Testament:
  - For Jesus and the Apostles, the "New Testament" was not a book, not words that were written down
  it was the Mass. And in the text of the New Testament, the phrase "New Testament" denotes not a
  text but an action, not a document but a sacrament.
  - Long before the New Testament was a document then, it was  a sacrament, established by Jesus
  and entrusted to His Church.
  - The Sacrament is prior to the document, according to the very words of the document

- **The Mass is the New Testament**
