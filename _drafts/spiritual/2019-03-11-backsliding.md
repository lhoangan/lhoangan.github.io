This is what struck me when I was a child listening to the Exodus story.
The Jewish people were freed from the slavery and miserable life in Egypt,
they've seen how happy it was to be rescued from the hands of Pharaoh, and how
the Egyptian army was defeated. However, throughout their journey across the
desert toward the Promised Land, they rebelled and for many times went against
Moses and the Lord.

Bishop Barron's homily on Bread of Life
[Word on Fire 8:00](https://wordonfire.podbean.com/e/bread-of-life-1466013652)
helps me figure out.

It's all very normal, and it all happens to each of us. Most of the time we
are trying to get away from a bad habit from abstaining from something bad,
but very often that we would fail back to the old way.

Much like how the people spending their life in struggles throughout the desert
to go to the Promised Land of Milk and Honey, we are walking through the desert
of life, going the the Eternal Promised Land of Heaven. Much like how God sending
manna from Heaven to substain the people, He's sending us the Eucharist, the
bread of Heaven, the supersubstainable bread to substain our faith and strength
in this life

The principle concern of this Gospel is to provide testimony to the enduring presence of Christ in the Eucharist—a presence which is the fulfillment of the ancient temple of Israel. The ark of the covenant contained the law of God and the manna from heaven, and was surrounded by the mysterious “showbread” or “bread of the presence.” Now, in the true tabernacles found in our churches, we find the living law, the true manna, and the definitive bread of the presence
