The reading today is about the prodigal son. It is a well-known story, and I've
heard several homilies on the topics, covering several (if not to say all) aspects
of the parable.

Yet it's still interesting to hear it again.

This part touched me deeply today.

When the second son comes back, the Father saw him first when he's still from 
afar: the Father respects the decision of the son so he let him goes but always
longs for him, making him always on guard and unceasingly watch out for him.

The reunite moment is touching: the son coming back smelly and all the weary
clothes, and dirty, but the Father first hold him in his arm and kiss him tenderly.
Despite all the speech that the son prepared, he just, as to say, doesn't give 
a damn, the next thing you find him to say is ordering the people to dress and
restore the son's position, a son. It feels like a magnifique happy-ending in
fairy tales, or a drama movie.

The first son lives with the Father but doesn't stay in his heart: he doesn't
know how and what the father think. Likewise, he stay with the father, but 
the father doesn't have a place in his heart: he obey him but out of order and
respect: "all these years I've been slaved to you". This reminds me, that
some times we, the good people, try to live within the law of God, but doesn't
feel love. Jesus said it doesn't matter if you keep the law, if you don't have
love that's nothing. the law of god is to protect us and keep us safe in his love
and so we are free to love him, not to enslave us, Are we living like a slave or
a son?

It also reminds me that some times it's good to behave like a child, to some times
court fondling/coddle from God who is our father. TO say to him like Martha says:
"You beloved is sick", 'You beloved son is needing you now"

> Ask and it will be given to you;
seek and you will find;
knock and the door will be opened to you.
For everyone who asks, receives; and the one who seeks, finds;
and to the one who knocks, the door will be opened.


It is quite a challenge for us to see how the paragraph could be true.
Surely we experience enough in our life to know that not all what we ask God
could turn into something real.

This is what I always tell myself, that the main point of my spiritual life is
if I belive or not. Nothing can happen if we just close our heart and walk away.
But as much difficult as it can be, if we take the first step toward God, He'll
not leave us aside. So the point is if we truly believe God is our Father, and
if He truly loves us and wants the best for us. If we do, then the next part
of the passage explain it more in depth

> Which one of you would hand his son a stone
when he asked for a loaf of bread,
or a snake when he asked for a fish?
If you then, who are wicked,
know how to give good gifts to your children,
how much more will your heavenly Father give good things
to those who ask him.

God is all powerful and there is nothing He cannot do. However, as He also
loves us infinitely He wants all the best for us. So despite how much we age in
life, in terms of enternity, we are just like a small toddler to God, and despite
how much we think we know about our life and desire, it is just like what a
toddler would want in the eyes of his father. Usually what we want is not what
we need: sometimes it is, some other times it could be of harm to us, some other
times it is but not at that moment.

So as long as we do believe that God is
omniscient, that He knows everything, we believe He knows us better than us,
and He knows what we really need at each certain moment in our lives. Thus, even
when we are not usually receiving what we ask in prayer, it is not the reason to
stop praying, but let's have faith and believe that might not be the best for us
and that God is working on whatever it is for our best and no matter what comes
after that be grateful, praise and thanks Him dearly for whatever it is, it is
the gift for us, and for the best of us. As it is said by Saint Therese of Lisieux
out of "Her Last COnversations"

> Everything is a grace, everything is the direct effect of our father's love -
difficulties, contradictions, humiliations, all the soul's miseries, her burdens,
her needs - everything, because through them, she learns humility, realizes her
weakness. Everything is a grace because everything is God's gift. Whatever be
the character of life or its unexpected events - to the heart that loves, all
is well.
