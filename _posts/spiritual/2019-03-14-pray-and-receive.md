> Ask and it will be given to you;
seek and you will find;
knock and the door will be opened to you.
For everyone who asks, receives; and the one who seeks, finds;
and to the one who knocks, the door will be opened.

I remeber how the passage puzzled me when I first heard it as a child. I did
not need to be an adult and experience much to know that not all that we pray
would turn into something real.  This was troubling because after all the
experiences that I had with praying as a child, I would never expect to see
something as direct and simple as this in the Bible. Very often when we hear
something from the Bible, we should see between the lines or there would be
some sort of "fuzzy condition" for them to be applicable. Such as when Jesus
teaches that faith as small as a mustard seed can even move a mountain, but how
to measure how large faith is, for just as soon as we start reasoning and re-thinking
about things, we fall right into the realm of doubtfulness.
So to the mind of a ten-year-old boy, surely there was more than just
saying a prayer to make things happen, and Jesus should not just say it simply
like that.

So it was quite a bother for me to take it in, but surprisingly the next part of
the passage was quite making sense:

> Which one of you would hand his son a stone
when he asked for a loaf of bread,
or a snake when he asked for a fish?
If you then, who are wicked,
know how to give good gifts to your children,
how much more will your heavenly Father give good things
to those who ask him.

This I'd experienced at home everyday. I knew very much that Mom and Dad love me, but
they just don't simply give me everything that I wanted and sometimes they even
went as far as hit me hard so that I learned my lesson and stopped doing bad things.
So as much as God loves us like a Father to a child, nothing we are receiving is
actually harmful or bad for us.

The main point is if we do believe that God is our Father, that He loves us as
a child, and that He knows everything, and can do absolutely anything?

For this I have to say that I did crazy things in my life.
I realized quite early in my life that despite all the ungranted prayers and
bad experiences that I had from praying, from going to church, and at Catechism
classes, I did have a quite of a good life.
I have a family, my family can afford my living, I have food for meals and
I was going school, a good school, indeed. It's true that I didn't always have
what I wanted, but who cares maybe that's how life is. After all I didn't always
receive what I wanted from mom and dad because they wanted me to earn it as a
reward or they knew that it could be harmful as I wasn't ready for it or it's
just bad. So, what if my faith was just actually short, what if God really
exists and all what He does are just to love me?
Surely a fourteen-year-old boy cannot answer all of that, but there was one thing
that I knew: I'd been neglecting in all aspects of my faith, I'd never truly
practiced it, I had no relationship with God. How can I know what He wants
for me, if I'm just standing there away from Him? So I decided to give it a shot,
I would just simply accept Him and all that I'd been taught to see how it goes.
After all I can always step back because I'd never been in.
That was crazy for me. And I'm glad that I was that crazy.

Pretty much like axioms in mathematics, you don't need to prove them, but once
you accept, you use them as premise for further reasoning and arguments. I
was not able to prove any of that, but by accepting them as the base, I found
my life much more meaningful. I guess it how's God works: He loves us from eternity
but He does not force His way into our heart but waits for us to respond. And
once you open your heart to Him, He'll never leave you aside. And that how the
passage apply to me: it is not just about praying and receiving, it's also about
how seeking and finding the truth, knocking and being open to infinite love and
grace. Once you open to God and accept Him as the premise of your life, you
realize that "*the power of prayer is the confidence that we are being guided and
cared for, even when that guidance and care are not immediately apparent*"
[Bishop Barron](https://www.facebook.com/BishopRobertBarron/photos/a.343034215735712/2166441740061608/?type=3&theater)

Praying is not to persuade God or to inform Him something you need and He does not
know. If you truly believe that God knows it all, you know that He knows
you better than you do to yourself, and He knows what is the best to you at each
moment in your life. If you truly believe that God loves you as a Father to a child,
you know that whatever you receive is the best for you, even when it does not feel
that way. When something unexpected comes across after a prayer, I would try to
regard it as a gift in unexpected wrapper, and try to understand what God is
telling me through it: it could be that I have not been ready for it, and that
it could bring harm to me or my souls to have at that moment, or maybe He's
preparing something even greater for me. Praying is not meant to change God, but
rather ourselves, it's "*the raising of the mind and heart to God*" (St. John
Damascene, De fide orth. 3,24). Through prayers we get closer to God, to understand
His plan for our life, to be like Him in daily life, and to love Him even more.

So if you are not receiving what you pray for, do not stop praying, but rather
pray more for you to make sense of what He gives you, prase and thanks Him for
with the believe that it's the best for you even when you don't see it. And be
like what Mother Mary does, "*Mary kept all these things, reflecting on them in
her heart*" [(Luke 2:19)](http://usccb.org/bible/luke/2:19)
